AI Strategy / Financial Governance

AI Unit Economics & Cost Governance

Model selection, token economics, and unit-of-work cost control-keeping AI adoption financially rational from workstation to product.

Pattern

AI is loved by developers, executives, and stakeholders alike-and its costs can outrun its value just as quickly, especially once embedded in products at scale.

Mechanism

An economic governance layer: model selection and benchmarking, token and fine-tuning economics, unit-of-work cost tracking, and scale-path evaluation with second-order consequences accounted for.

Outcome

Adoption stays financially rational at scale. Cost of AI is governed per unit of work, scale decisions are made with full consequence accounting, and early adoption still generates market revenue.

Executive Summary

Every organization wants AI; very few can say what a unit of AI work costs them. Without that number, adoption either stalls on fear or scales on enthusiasm-and both failure modes end the same way, with a bill that outruns the value.

Built the economic governance layer for AI adoption: model selection and efficiency benchmarking run internally rather than assumed from vendor claims; token and implementation costs tracked per use case; fine-tuning trade-offs analyzed deliberately; and unit-of-work cost controlled for AI embedded in products-precisely where costs scale out of control fastest. Scale paths were evaluated with full consequence accounting, including self-hosting options weighed for control, IP protection, and cost against maintenance burden and the risk of obsolescence. Early adoption was pursued deliberately: market integration through partnerships generates revenue that funds the learning curve while the economics stay governed.

Problem Recognized

  • AI costs compound quietly-token by token, request by request-until they surface as a budget crisis.
  • Product-embedded AI scales costs with usage, and usage is the goal.
  • Vendor benchmarks describe vendor scenarios, not your workloads.
  • Self-hosting looks like control and savings until maintenance, staffing, and obsolescence are counted.

Strategic Position

  • If you cannot price the unit of work, you cannot scale the work.
  • Model choice is an economic decision first and a technical one second.
  • Every scale decision carries first- and second-order consequences-account for them before committing.
  • Early adoption is an investment: partnerships and market integration fund the learning curve.

Leadership Mechanisms

Unit-of-Work Cost Model

Cost tracked per unit of work for AI embedded in products-the place where costs scale out of control fastest.

Model Selection & Benchmarking

Models selected and efficiency-benchmarked internally, per use case, against real workloads rather than vendor claims.

Token & Fine-Tuning Economics

Token costs and fine-tuning trade-offs evaluated deliberately for each use case, including implementation and adoption cost.

Self-Host vs. API Evaluation

Self-hosting evaluated for control, IP protection, and cost mitigation-then weighed against maintenance burden and the fear of falling behind.

Second-Order Consequence Analysis

Scale decisions account for first- and second-order effects: staffing, maintenance, obsolescence risk, and optionality lost.

Adoption-Revenue Loop

Early adoption pursued deliberately-market integration through partnerships generates creative revenue sources that fund the learning curve.

Transferable Operating Principles

  • Enthusiasm is not a cost model.
  • Product-embedded AI needs unit economics before it needs scale.
  • Benchmark on your own workloads or you are borrowing someone else's confidence.
  • Control has a carrying cost-self-hosting is a balance sheet decision.
  • Obsolescence risk is real: sometimes the expensive option is standing still.
  • Adoption funded by market revenue beats adoption funded by budget anxiety.
